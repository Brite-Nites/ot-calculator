What this does not count — read before quoting a number
  1. What the words mean. Employer burden — what you pay on top of wages; your cost, not part of their check. Worker deductions — what comes out of their check. Real cost per hour (blended) — total wages divided by hours worked, so it shows what you are actually paying per hour once overtime is mixed in. With burden — wages plus the employer add-ons. Hours worked — not shift length; meal breaks are unpaid, so a 16-hour shift with two 30-minute breaks is 15 paid hours.
  2. Both percentages are placeholders. Employer burden defaults to 7.65%, which is the employer FICA share only (6.2% Social Security + 1.45% Medicare) — statutory, and it excludes unemployment and workers' comp. Workers' comp is the expensive one for roof work, so the real figure is higher. Worker deductions default to 18.2%, drawn from observed payroll at roughly 82% net-to-gross; it varies by person, filing status and state. Overwrite both once you have real numbers.
  3. "Federal" covers every state without a daily overtime rule — VA, UT, AZ, WA, MD, DC, NC, PA, NY, CT, FL and TX among Brite's territories. Only California and Colorado have daily rules.
  4. Wage cost only. Not included: California meal and rest break premiums (one hour of pay per violation, and shifts over 10 hours require a second meal period), productivity decline on long days, safety exposure, or turnover.
  5. A 10% productivity loss erases the whole cheap-labour-plus-overtime advantage. $1,800 ÷ $25.00 = 72 effective hours. If 80 nominal hours produce fewer than 72 hours of real output, an $18 worker at 80 hours no longer beats a $25 worker at 40.
  6. Enter hours worked, not shift length. Meal breaks are unpaid when properly taken, so a 16-hour shift with two 30-minute meals is 15 paid hours. In California that's $396 against $360 at $18/hr — $36 a day.
  7. Splitting hours evenly across days understates California and Colorado cost, always. Double-time and daily overtime trigger on individual long days, and averaging minimises how many hours clear those lines. Measured: 66 hours over 6 days is $1,422 evenly split against $1,476 ragged — 3.8% low. Use the per-day boxes for real weeks.
  8. Colorado's 12-consecutive-hours rule is not modelled. Colorado owes overtime after 12 consecutive hours even when they span two calendar days. That needs shift start and end times, not daily totals.
  9. This page cannot track law changes. It is self-contained with no connection to anything. What moves every January 1: the California minimum wage. Law monitoring is a service — your payroll provider, an employment attorney, or CalChamber alerts.
  10. Validation. The federal path is checked against 29 periods of real Brite payroll, 26 matching to the cent. California and Colorado are built from published state rules and checked against hand-computed cases only — no Brite paycheck exists to test them against.
